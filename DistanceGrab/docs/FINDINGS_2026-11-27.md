## F1: A clip you cannot use is carried in the hand; only guns, health and armour get the FULL word
- **OWNER 2026-11-27: "Say AMMO FULL, drop it". FIXED (flight.zs FullWord: refused ammo answers AMMO FULL). NOT YET COMPILED OR RUN.**
- Kind: possible bug
- Where: flight.zs:674-696 (the refusals TakeInto names), flight.zs:423 and :437 (Catch: only a named refusal drops it at your feet; everything else goes to the carry); near.zs:318-325 (the walk-up grip, the same); old README line 36 (the picture's caption: "A clip pulled to the hand with the gun already full: AMMO FULL")
- What the code does: when the game refuses a pickup, the catch says AMMO FULL only if the pickup gave a gun you own, and HEALTH FULL / ARMOR FULL only for a Health or Armor item. Any other refused pickup (a clip or shell box with that ammo at its maximum, a key you already hold, a powerup the game will not take) sets no refusal. It is carried: it floats in front of the hand until the palm turns back, then drops or is thrown.
- Why it looks wrong: the owner's rule in the code (flight.zs:414-422) is that what you cannot use is not taken and says so. Pulling ammo with full ammo is the common case, and it gives a clip stuck in front of your hand and no word. The old README's own picture caption says a clip gives AMMO FULL.
- Sure?: likely. Certain about the code path; that the game refuses ammo at its maximum is the engine's rule. A rig test settles it: full bullets, pull a clip.
- Fix A: in TakeInto, a refused Ammo sets the refusal with AMMO FULL, so a pull puts it at your feet and a walk-up leaves it (about 6 lines, flight.zs).
- Fix B: every refused pickup, whatever its kind, is dropped at your feet with no word (about 3 lines, flight.zs).
- Or intended: the README keeps its row: any pickup the game would not take is carried, so you can take it somewhere or throw it.

## F3: A pull from close by leaves about 0.3 seconds to get the palm back out for the catch
- Kind: other (an input that may be too fast)
- Where: grab.zs:463-469 (the pull only starts when the palm LEAVES the palm-out pose), flight.zs:1101-1104 (flight time: distance at 22 m/s, never under 6 tics), flight.zs:1014-1022 and :1040 (the catch needs the hand armed again; 4 tics on the palm, then it drops), CVARINFO.txt:172, :301
- What the code does: the flick is read on the tic the palm stops being out. The thing then flies for 6 tics if it is within 128 units (about 3.8 m), up to 24 tics at full Reach. It is caught only if the palm is out again when it arrives or in the 4 tics after. So the palm must go out of its roll band and back in within about 10 tics (0.29 s) for a near thing, about 28 tics (0.8 s) for a far one. A miss drops it under the palm: a pickup is usually walked over anyway, a barrel lands at your feet.
- Why it looks wrong: the near case is the one a new player tries first, and it has the shortest window. Nothing on screen says the palm has to come back.
- Sure?: unsure. A wrist that only wobbles out of the band for a tic during the flick is back in time by itself, which may be what happens in the headset. A headset test settles it: pull a barrel from 2 m and from 12 m, ten times each, count the catches.
- Fix A: raise the default Catch Window from 4 tics to about 12 (1 line, CVARINFO.txt; saved settings keep 4 unless a settings generation moves them, about 4 more lines in aim.zs Migrate).
- Fix B: a pulled thing that arrives is caught whether or not the palm is out, unless the hand has turned to another thing (about 6 lines, flight.zs).
- Or intended: the README keeps its two rows: turn the palm out again before it arrives; leave it turned away and it drops.

## F4: The tick when a door commits is always in the main hand, whichever hand lifted it

- Fix A: send the hand with the release event (a third argument) and tick that hand (about 4 lines, door.zs).
- Or intended: none; the comment states the aim and the code misses it.
